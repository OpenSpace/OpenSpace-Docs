## Overview

The Nearby Stars is, more formally, the Gaia Catalog of Nearby Stars (GCNS). Gaia is a revolutionary mission to get precise information on the positions and motions of billions of stars. See the index page on [Stars](content--milky-way--stars--gaia) for more information on the Gaia mission and data release.

## Limitations of the Nearby Stars

With over 300,000 stars, the GCNS provides the most complete picture of stars within 326 light years of the Sun, with a few important caveats. This is a *limited* sample of stars, but is a complete catalog within these following limitations.


### Cool stars limitation

The coolest stars grow too faint to see beyond 100 parsecs, so the first limitation is the distance cutoff so that we can have a complete sample that includes the cooler, dimmer stars. Gaia DR3 has 95% of all stars down to spectral type M8; stars cooler than this are not fully represented in this data set.


### Bright stars limitation

Many of the bright stars we see in our night sky are too bright for Gaia's normal observation mode. These stars must be handled differently by the telescope. Many of the stars you are familiar with in the sky are abselt from Gaia, for now. Gaia's Data Release 4 will include some of these bright stars, and the remaining will be included in Data Release 5, expected around 2030.


### Binary and multiple star systems

Confusion can rein when it comes to deriving quality data from two stars whose light mingles with each other. Accounting for binary systems takes further analysis, some of which is present in this data set, but more refined data will arrive in subsequent data releases from Gaia.
